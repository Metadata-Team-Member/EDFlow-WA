How long do people wait to be seen in a WA ED?

Each marker is one invented emergency department attendance. It arrives at a real WA site, waits in the lane for its triage category against the time-to-treatment target, is seen, and leaves: admitted, home, or transferred. Rings show the stage: none while waiting, one in care, two when ready to leave. The map and sites are real reference data; everything that happens to the markers is made up.

What the measures mean

Seen within target: time from arrival to the commencement of clinical care, taken as the earlier of Doctor Seen Datetime and Nurse Seen Datetime (the rule in the WA Health 2025-26 OBM KPI Data Definition Manual; DD 2026 pp.30, 66) against the ATS target (BR §7.4). People who did not wait or were referred at triage are not in the denominator (open question 7).

4 hours, 6 hours: length of stay from arrival to departure. These references come from the WA Emergency Access Measures named in BR §12 but are not given in the EDDC documents; they are shown for discussion and must be confirmed (open question 1). The virtual ED (WAVED) is left out of length-of-stay measures.

All figures are synthetic and say nothing about real performance.

All activity (people, attendances, times, outcomes, counts) is invented by a random-number generator so the design can be tested before any real extract is requested. Nothing here should be quoted as a finding about WA Health, any service or any person. Aligned to the WA Health Emergency Department Data Collection Data Dictionary 2026, Data Specifications, July 2026, Emergency Department Patient Activity Data Business Rules, July 2026 and the Establishment List, May 2026; the sites shown are exactly those marked “Reports to EDDC” = Y. How to read this

  • One marker = one ED attendance. Every marker is the same friendly, gender-neutral shape with a leaf sprout, the same size for everyone. It stands for an attendance, not a person.
  • Rings = stage. No ring while waiting to be seen, one ring once clinical care has started, two rings when the person is ready to leave the ED (for example a bed has been requested). Rings say nothing about how unwell someone is.
  • Colour is the triage category by default (ATS 1 red to ATS 5 white). You can colour by presenting complaint group, Health Service Provider or mode of arrival instead.
  • Panel 1, the map. Real WA health regions with a Perth metro inset. Each dot is an EDDC site (“Reports to EDDC” = Y), coloured by Health Service Provider; dots are one size per site type, not by beds or volume. The WA Virtual ED (WAVED) has its own “Virtual” badge. A marker appears at its site when it arrives.
  • Panel 2, waiting to be seen. One lane per triage category. Position = time waited; the dashed line is the BR §7.4 target (2 / 10 / 30 / 60 / 120 minutes) and the lane runs on to 12 times the target. Past the target, a marker gets a red outline and a concerned face. People who did not wait leave from here with a “did not wait” label.
  • Panel 3, in the ED. One block per Health Service Provider. Doors are treatment areas (Resus, Acute, Fast track, Mental health, Paediatric, Virtual): illustrative only, as EDDC has no treatment-area field. On the floor, markers drift right with time since arrival; the amber dashed line is 4 hours and the right edge is 12 hours or more. Markers leave through a gate: ward, home or transfer.
  • Spotlight (last block): hover or click a site dot, or click a row in the busiest-sites list, to see one site.
  • Markers shown: by default 1 in 2 attendances are drawn on a desktop (1 in 3 on a phone) so the scene stays readable; choose “Every attendance” to see them all. Numbers and charts always use every invented attendance.

What real data we'd need

This prototype runs on invented data shaped like the EDDC extract (Data Specifications, July 2026, Appendix B). To swap in real data, the key items are:

  • Attendance fields: establishment code, arrival date/time, triage date/time, triage category, presenting complaint code, mode of arrival, visit type, referral source, doctor seen and nurse seen date/time, departure ready date/time, bed request and ward ready date/time, departure date/time, departure status, destination, stream.
  • The presenting complaint code list with descriptions, so complaints can be grouped properly.
  • De-identified person fields: age group only. No name, record number or date of birth is needed for this view.
  • Reference data: an official site to Health Service Provider and region mapping, and confirmation of the 4-hour and admission targets in use.
  • Governance: approval through the WA Health data custodian, small-number suppression, and Aboriginal data governance.
